## Context

DevSteward's account/quota provider (`CswapAccountProvider`, `core/accounts.py`) hand-rolls a
**per-account** budget gate: `precheck()` reads cswap's cached `usage.json` directly, computes
each slot's 5h/7d %, and stays / switches / waits at every step boundary against a fixed
per-account threshold (default 70%, REQ-025). It refuses to admit a job when the *active*
account is low even if the other accounts could collectively carry it, and it owns a second
copy of the usage-parsing + cswap-switch logic.

The sibling project **clauder** (`/home/peter/projects/clauder`) already exists for exactly this
problem and is **built** (its REQ-001…004 are done): it is the budget/account **policy layer**
over cswap. cswap stays the mechanism (credentials, raw usage, `--switch-to`); clauder is the
policy that decides *proceed / switch / wait* against the **combined** remaining budget of all
accounts and tells cswap when to switch. It exposes three commands:

- `clauder gate --threshold T [--use U] --json` — the **delegation seam**: one admission verdict
  over the pooled 5h budget (`proceed` / `switch` / `wait` / `unsatisfiable`), picks the best
  account to enter on, computes wait durations, and performs the switch. Exit codes 0/0/75/69.
- `clauder monitor --interval N --threshold T` — a continuous loop that pre-emptively
  `cswap --switch-to`es as the active account's 5h window saturates.
- `clauder usage --json` — a per-account + combined snapshot.

This REQ makes the engine **delegate its budget gate to clauder** instead of reaching into
cswap itself — adopting clauder's more granular, combined-budget policy and deleting DevSteward's
duplicate cswap machinery. It treats clauder as an **external black-box tool** (the encapsulation
principle, `[[devsteward-is-a-black-box-to-agents]]` applied the other way: DevSteward consumes
clauder only through its CLI contract): the provider shells `clauder gate`, reads its JSON/exit
code, and never touches cswap or `usage.json` directly.

Three facts from the owner shape the design:

- **The engine never owns the monitor.** clauder's continuous `monitor` runs out-of-band — the
  operator starts it manually or as a service. The engine does **not** spawn or manage it. But it
  still calls `clauder gate` at **every step boundary**, so budget is enforced even when no
  monitor is running. API-query frequency is unpredictable, so a per-step gate is the robust
  call; the monitor is a live optimisation the operator runs separately.
- **No race, ever.** A background `clauder monitor` and the engine-invoked `clauder gate` are
  both clauder processes that can call `cswap --switch-to`; they must never corrupt the switch.
  The only place to serialise that is **inside clauder** (a lock around the switch). DevSteward's
  contribution is to add **no second switcher**: route all account interaction through the single
  `clauder gate` chokepoint and never call cswap directly. The clauder-side lock is an external
  prerequisite (a clauder REQ), not a DevSteward `depends_on`.
- **A running `claude -p` can't be rescued mid-session.** Switching cswap edits `~/.claude.json`,
  which only affects the *next* launch — it cannot move an in-flight session. So a step that
  saturates mid-run still surfaces as `USAGE_LIMIT` and re-queues (REQ-016) onto the now-best
  account; the gate's job is to *enter* each step on a healthy account, and the background monitor
  pre-positions between calls. This is why the boundary gate — not a mid-session rescue — is the
  engine's mechanism.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|----------|-----------|
| 1 | **Delegate the per-step budget gate to the `clauder gate` CLI (subprocess).** A new `ClauderAccountProvider.precheck()` runs `clauder gate --threshold T --json` at each step launch and acts on the verdict/exit code: `proceed`/`switch` (0) → launch on the chosen account; `wait` (75, `wait_seconds`) → sleep interruptibly and re-gate; `unsatisfiable` (69) → return `ok=False` (the run stops). clauder reads usage and performs any switch. | Q1. Matches clauder's delegation-seam design (flags in, JSON/exit-code answer); adopts the combined-budget policy; treats clauder as a black-box external tool — no Python dependency between the packages. |
| 2 | **Gate at every step boundary; never manage the monitor.** clauder's `monitor` runs out-of-band (operator-started, manually or as a service); the engine neither spawns nor supervises it, but calls `clauder gate` on every step so budget is enforced with or without a monitor. | Owner's call: the monitor may or may not be up; per-step checking is robust because API-query cadence is unpredictable. The boundary gate is the safety net; the monitor is a separate live optimisation. |
| 3 | **Route all account interaction through the single `clauder` chokepoint — never call cswap directly — and add no second switcher.** The provider issues exactly one `clauder gate` per step launch and does no independent usage-reading or switching. | Owner's hard constraint: no race between the background monitor and the engine's gate. Serialising the actual switch is clauder's job (a lock); DevSteward's part is to present one indirect chokepoint, so it contributes no concurrent switch of its own. |
| 4 | **Drop the direct-cswap machinery (subtraction).** Remove `CswapAccountProvider`'s per-account precheck loop, `usage_snapshot`/`AccountUsage`, and all direct `cswap`/`usage.json` reads; clauder owns budget policy now. When clauder is absent, degrade to "proceed without quota check" (today's cswap-absent spirit) rather than falling back to a second gate. `SingleAccountProvider` stays for tests/minimal setups. | Q3. One budget code path, not two; the combined-budget policy lives in exactly one place (clauder). Keeping a parallel cswap gate would defeat the single-chokepoint and re-duplicate the logic this REQ removes. |
| 5 | **The slot-pin (`use=N`, REQ-025 D6) is dropped; clauder's combined-budget selection supersedes per-account pinning.** clauder `gate` admits on the pooled budget and picks the best entry account; there is no "pin to slot N". | Combined-budget is strictly more capable than pinning a single account; preserving pin-mode would re-introduce the per-account concept clauder replaces. *(Flagged for the owner — revisit if slot-pinning is still wanted.)* |
| 6 | **Real-environment behaviour is a `manual` AC; the delegation wiring is `regression` against a stubbed clauder.** Whether the integration actually keeps sessions alive across switches and stays race-free is observable only against real cswap + ≥2 accounts + a live monitor (a human oracle); the verdict handling, the single-chokepoint, and the degradation are coupled-oracle regression. | §2a: a mocked-clauder green proves wiring, not reality. Account-switching against live quota needs a live sign-off; the wiring does not. |

## Requirement

Replace the engine's direct-cswap budget gate with a provider that delegates to clauder's
combined-budget `gate` at every step boundary, treating clauder as an optional external tool and
adding no second account switcher.

- A `ClauderAccountProvider` shells `clauder gate --threshold T --json` at each step launch and
  maps the verdict/exit code to the existing `precheck() -> (ok, reason)` contract: `proceed`/
  `switch` → `ok=True` (launch); `wait` → sleep `wait_seconds` interruptibly (honouring a stop)
  then re-gate; `unsatisfiable` → `ok=False` (stop the run). The configured threshold is passed
  through to clauder.
- The provider performs **no** direct `cswap` call and reads **no** `usage.json`: all account
  interaction goes through the single `clauder gate` invocation. The per-account precheck loop and
  cswap usage-parsing are removed.
- When `clauder` is not on PATH, `precheck()` degrades to `(True, "proceeding without quota
  check")` without raising — clauder is an optional external tool, discovered on PATH like cswap.
- The engine does not start, stop, or supervise `clauder monitor`; that runs out-of-band.

## Notes

- **clauder readiness — an external prerequisite, tracked in clauder's own ledger.** clauder is
  built (REQ-001…004 done) and exposes `gate`/`monitor`/`usage`, but at survey time it has an
  uncommitted change in `clauder/monitor.py`, is still on the pre-REQ-047 feature-branch model,
  and — critically — does **not** yet guarantee race-free concurrent invocation (no lock around
  the cswap switch shared by a `monitor` and a `gate`). AC3 (the race-free live validation)
  depends on a **clauder-side lock REQ**, which the owner intakes in clauder's repo — it cannot be
  a DevSteward `depends_on`. AC1/AC2 (stubbed clauder) land independently of clauder's state.
- **Why the lock lives in clauder, not here.** The two racing processes are both clauder
  (`monitor` + engine-invoked `gate`); only clauder can serialise the cswap switch they share.
  DevSteward's contribution is to add no second switcher (Decision 3). Solving the race in
  DevSteward would be the wrong layer (a house design principle).
- **clauder stays an optional external tool, not a bundled dependency.** It is discovered on PATH
  like cswap; DevSteward does not add `clauder` to its package dependencies, keeping the two
  pipx-editable packages decoupled.
- **`--use` semantics differ between the tools.** DevSteward's old `use=N` was a *slot pin*;
  clauder's `--use=N` is *expected burn percent*. This REQ drops the slot pin (Decision 5) and
  does not currently pass a burn estimate (`--use` defaults to 0); wiring a per-step burn estimate
  is a possible future refinement, not in scope.
- **Process declarations:** fused develop, no concept phase. AC1/AC2 are `regression`
  (coupled oracles — the verdict mapping, the single-chokepoint, the degradation); AC3 is `manual`
  (live human oracle). The System-Test phase therefore exists, but its surface is the operator's
  own cswap + multi-account environment — there is no owned fixture asset to build, so `process.lab`
  is empty and the block is omitted.
- **Localization:** all provider messages are operator-facing CLI/technical English; no localized
  UI strings.
