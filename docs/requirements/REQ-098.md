## Context

The owner's need, in the owner's words (backlog item `simpler-state-changes`): *"Steward's
state changes are overly rigid: recently I had to run a validation I already knew would fail,
because I cannot rework without a failed validation. I want only the two moves I actually
use: repeat the same step, or go back to the previous step."*

What happened is exact. A REQ's develop step was `done` and its validate step was waiting —
never started, or parked on a `manual`-AC hold (state F). The owner already knew the
validation would go red (the develop was hollow, or the acceptance fixture was wrong) and
wanted the develop step back. The only verb that reopens develop is `steward rework`, and it
refuses unless the ledger holds a *real* red — a `validation` event with `ok: false` **and**
the validate step `BLOCKED` on it (`lifecycle._resolve_red_validation`). So the owner ran a
validation whose outcome was known, paid for a System-Tester session or walked a manual
procedure to record the red, and only then could type the verb they wanted in the first place.

That guard is not an accident; it is the recorded design. REQ-033 Decision 3 scoped `rework`
to *"in-flight reds only, both kinds"*, and REQ-055 Decision 2 made `revalidate` match it:
*"`revalidate` requires a **real red** validation, exactly as `rework` does … **Peter's
choice: red-only, symmetric to `rework`.** The validate-parked-**pending** state (state F —
a manual AC awaiting its human oracle, *no* validation event) is **not** revalidate's job; its
forward path stays `steward validate REQ` attended."* The owner made that choice with the
red-validation case in view and without the pending case in hand. Living with it has shown the
guard protects nothing: the red it demands carries no information the owner does not already
have, and the state-F forward path it preserves (`steward validate`) is the one the owner is
trying *not* to take. This REQ revisits that decision with the owner present.

The second half of the item — *only the two moves* — is a count. The recovery surface today
has four return/re-run verbs, each keyed to one ledger shape the operator must recognise
before choosing: `repeat` (a `FAILED` step), `rework` (validate `BLOCKED` on a red — back to
develop), `revalidate` (the same red — re-run validate only, carrying the green ACs), and
`reland` (validate `FAILED` by a land-gate refusal after a green — replay the land without a
session). The STEWARD.md Recovery table is a seven-row state→verb lookup, and `steward status`,
the red-park brief, the `validate-record` routing message and the dashboard's `_offer` each
re-derive it. The operator's actual vocabulary has two words: *again*, and *back*. Which body
those words need — carry the greens, replay the land, clear a hold, re-arm a failed step — is a
fact the ledger already holds, so the engine can read it rather than make the human name it.

REQ-097 Decision 5 anticipated exactly this REQ: the dashboard exposes *"exactly the CLI's
action set, with the CLI's preconditions and refusals"* so that *"a later change to the state
machine (`simpler-state-changes`) [can] flow through without touching the dashboard's
contract."* The verb set shrinks here; the dashboard follows through `verbs.py` with no
transition of its own. One usability change rides along on that page (Decision 8): the owner
asked for the Backlog section to sit above the Requirements section on a project page, since
the backlog is what the owner acts on there and the requirements list is read-only.

## Requirement

### The two moves

The operator's recovery vocabulary is two verbs, and the engine — not the operator — reads the
ledger to decide what each one does:

- **`steward repeat REQ-NNN`** — *run the same step again.* Re-arms `REQ-NNN`'s stopped step(s)
  from whatever stopped them (Decision 3).
- **`steward rework REQ-NNN [--reason "…"]`** — *go back to the previous step.* Returns
  `REQ-NNN:validate` to `REQ-NNN:develop` from any state except done (Decisions 2, 4).

`steward revalidate` and `steward reland` no longer exist (Decision 1). The forward verbs
(`steward validate`, `steward run`, `steward checkpoint`, `/advance`) and the fork verbs
(`steward decide`, `steward decision-answer`) are unchanged.

### `rework`

Given a REQ with a validate step whose develop step is `DONE`:

- validate `PENDING` (never started) → develop `RECOVER`, validate `PENDING`, a `rework` event
  with `brief` = the `--reason` or `returned to develop by the operator`, no `evidence`, no
  `scope`.
- validate `BLOCKED` on a manual hold (no validation event) → the same; the hold is gone.
- validate `BLOCKED` on a red → exactly today's behaviour (evidence, red brief, scope and
  carried greens with provenance, any parked validate decision answered), with `--reason`
  replacing the brief when given.
- validate `FAILED` → the same as the pending case; the failed attempt's partial tree is left
  as it is (the `repeat` doctrine).

Refusals (`LifecycleError`, non-zero, no mutation): a `done` REQ (→ supersede), no validate
step, develop not `DONE`, validate `RUNNING`.

### `repeat`

- Any `FAILED` step of the REQ → `RECOVER`, `step_recover` event — unless it is a validate
  step whose last events are a green validation followed by a `land_refused`, in which case the
  recorded green is replayed and the REQ lands (the former `reland`), refusing if the land gate
  is still red.
- validate `BLOCKED` on a red → `PENDING`, the green ACs carried forward with provenance, the
  parked validate decision answered, a scope-bearing `revalidate` event (the former
  `revalidate`); the CLI prints what is carried and what re-opens, as `rework` does today.
- validate `BLOCKED` on a manual hold → `PENDING`, the hold cleared, no event scope (a full
  run).

Refusals: the REQ has no stopped step (every step `DONE`, `PENDING` or `RUNNING`).

### Surfaces

- `steward status` names `steward repeat` on a `FAILED` step and `steward repeat` /
  `steward rework` on a held or red validate step, beside the hold's own `steward validate`
  hint; the red-park brief, the `validate-record` routing message and the `reland` refusal text
  no longer name `revalidate` or `reland`.
- The dashboard offers exactly the CLI's set: `repeat` and `rework` on a failed, held or red
  step; `rework` on a pending validate step whose develop is done; the rework form carries a
  reason field; the `verb/<verb>` route rejects `revalidate` and `reland`.
- `STEWARD.md`, the `/advance` skill and the handbook describe the two moves and nothing else.

## Notes

**Out of scope, deliberately.** A "go back" from the develop step — there is no step before it
in the fused model (REQ-029); a develop that must be redone is redone in place. Reopening a
`done` REQ — REQ-001's invariant stands, supersede instead. A `--reason` on `repeat` — not
asked for; the tree and the ledger already carry the failed attempt's context. Renaming the
internal `RECOVER`/`PENDING` symbols or the ledger event names (Decision 5).

**Risks.** (1) The REQ-089 Decision 7 stale-sign-off hazard is unchanged in shape: a `repeat`
of a red validate carries every green forward, as `revalidate` did, and the defence is the same
provenance and the `scoped re-run (REQ-075): …` announcement. A `rework` from a hold or a
pending step carries nothing, so it adds no new exposure. (2) After a land refusal, `repeat`
replays the land rather than re-running the validation; a fresh full validation is reached by
`rework` and forward again (Decision 3). (3) `repeat` on a REQ with *both* a failed develop and
a stopped validate re-arms both, as today's `repeat` re-arms every failed step — the executor's
dependency order runs develop first.

**Consumer sweep — the named home (Decision 9).** Right after this REQ lands: run
`steward sync` in each stamped consumer to refresh STEWARD.md and the skills, and sweep the
non-synced docs that name `steward revalidate` / `steward reland` by hand (at intake:
flowsteward 4 files, DriveSteward 1; the 2026-08-30 `decision-list` sweep is the pattern).
Until then a stale runbook command fails with click's *no such command* — loud, not silent.

**Design record revisited.** This REQ withdraws the "real red" precondition of REQ-033
Decision 3 and REQ-055 Decision 2 with the owner present (quoted in Context). It does not touch
REQ-030 Decision 8 (the engine still never spawns a repair session on a red) or REQ-033
Decision 4 (every rework cycle still costs an explicit human verb).
