## Context

Peter's standing feedback (2026-07-03 intake brief §3,
`docs/reports/2026-07-03-ledger-lost-update-and-decision-recovery-gap.md`): he has used
`steward decision` only a few times and each time felt lost as a user. Three strands:

1. **Missing context.** `decision list` prints id, step, and question — but not the
   situation: what was tried, what the options mean, what each choice would *do*. The
   operator is handed a question with no briefing.
2. **An answer is not a decision.** The `Decision` model has an `options` field that
   nothing ever populates; `decision answer` takes a free-text string. The operator cannot
   *choose* among defined branches — they type prose into a box, with no mechanical link
   between what they type and what the engine then does.
3. **Rarely a useful effect.** Most parks are either `:validate` holds (refused and
   redirected) or the mechanical states REQ-056 already ruled should not be decisions;
   answering frequently either refuses or flips `BLOCKED → PENDING` only to re-run and
   re-park.

This matches the doctrine on record: `decisions-are-real-forks-only` (REQ-056) drained
the mechanical parks, and the 2026-07-02 process review §1.2 found only **2 of 34**
historical parked decisions were genuine design forks. The mechanism is now used almost
entirely for things it was not designed to resolve, and the UX reflects that mismatch.

The premise question — does `steward decision` earn its surface at all? — must be
answered **before** touching the command. The two directions (intake brief §4.3):

- **(a) Reshape into a real fork tool:** populate `options` at park time, let the
  operator select one, carry the briefing context needed to choose, drive a concrete
  engine action per option.
- **(b) Shrink to match real usage:** after REQ-056 almost nothing legitimately parks;
  keep `list` + the redirect guards, question whether the free-text answer path survives
  at all. Subtraction-first.

Intake's lean is **(b)** — it aligns with `decisions-are-real-forks-only`, with the
2-of-34 evidence, and with "prefer subtraction" — but the call is Peter's, and shrinking
has a real cost to check first: the free-text answer is today the only channel by which
an unattended skill-question fork (park-and-surface §4) hands the operator's guidance to
the resuming session. A shrink must inventory the *genuine* remaining producers before
deleting their consumer.

## Requirement

When this REQ is done, the `steward decision` surface matches its real job: every parked
decision an operator sees is a genuine fork, presented with enough context to act on, and
acting on it has a concrete, predictable effect. The concrete behaviour — reshape (options
+ briefing + per-option action) or shrink (a smaller surface; genuine producers routed to
real verbs) — is fixed by the concept deliverable, which also replaces the provisional
acceptance below with direction-specific criteria before build.
